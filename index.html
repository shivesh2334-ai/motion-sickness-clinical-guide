02 / INVESTIGATIONS

Test the clinical concern

Typical episodes: no routine tests

A recurrent pattern of nausea, pallor or sweating during travel, resolving after exposure, is usually assessed clinically. Do not order routine CT, MRI or vestibular testing for this pattern alone.

Examine first

Check vitals and hydration; consider orthostatic BP. Examine gait, eye movements, neurological signs and hearing when symptoms are atypical. Review migraine, medicines and pregnancy possibility.

HINTS is for trained clinicians evaluating the appropriate acute vestibular syndrome; it is not a self-test or a general motion sickness screen.

Selective tests

  • Repeated vomiting / dehydration: electrolytes, renal function and glucose as indicated.
  • Pregnancy test when clinically relevant, with consent.
  • Palpitations, syncope or QT-risk medicines: ECG as indicated.
  • Hearing changes: audiometry / ENT review.
  • Suspected central disease: urgent stroke / neurology pathway; imaging chosen by the evaluating team.
These are clinician-selected investigations for alternative diagnoses or complications, not a mandatory panel.

03 / MANAGEMENT

Prevention comes first

Before the journey

Sleep adequately, avoid alcohol and choose a seat with less movement: near aircraft wings or the middle of a ship. Keep the head supported; look towards a stable distant view. Avoid reading and screens.

During symptoms

Reduce or stop the provoking activity safely, get fresh air, rest and use small frequent sips of fluid / ORS. Ongoing vomiting may prevent oral absorption; clinician-directed treatment and hydration may be needed.

Ginger has uncertain benefit; acupressure bands have not reliably outperformed placebo.

Adult reference only. Select one suitable agent after checking contraindications and the local label. Do not combine sedating drugs or add single ingredients already present in a fixed combination. No driving or alcohol.

Spinfree & similar combinations

Spinfree contains cinnarizine 20 mg + dimenhydrinate 40 mg. The Indian prescribing information indicates treatment of vertigo, so this app does not automatically recommend it for uncomplicated motion sickness.

For clinician reference, its vertigo label uses one tablet three times daily after food for no more than four weeks. Avoid in severe renal impairment (CrCl <25 mL/min), severe hepatic disease, angle-closure glaucoma or urinary retention. Pregnancy safety is not established; avoid during breastfeeding. Not a pediatric travel regimen. Check the full label and interactions.

Medicines that do not replace vestibular prevention

Ondansetron and newer non-sedating antihistamines are ineffective for motion sickness. Betahistine and pregnancy nausea combinations are not default travel-sickness treatments. Stimulants are not recommended to counteract sedation.

Special populations

Children, pregnant or breastfeeding patients, older adults and people with significant comorbidities need individualized review. The app deliberately generates no pediatric or pregnancy prescription.

05 / REASSESSMENT

Close the loop

After an episode

  • Confirm improvement once the provoking motion ends.
  • Check hydration, intake and urine output after vomiting.
  • Review sedation, blurred vision, confusion, urinary difficulty or abnormal movements.
  • Arrange review for persistent symptoms, new susceptibility or recurrence without travel.

Before the next journey

  • Record trigger, duration and functional impact.
  • Review which positioning measures helped.
  • Check medicine, dose, timing and adverse effects with the clinician.
  • Discuss a supervised suitability check / trial before travel where appropriate; do not drive during a trial.
  • Consider vestibular / migraine evaluation for atypical recurrent episodes.

Escalate immediately

New neurological symptoms, collapse, severe headache, inability to walk, blood in vomit or severe dehydration need urgent assessment. Sudden hearing loss also needs prompt care.
